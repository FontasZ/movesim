movesim is made by Xenofontas (Fontas) Dimitropoulos, a professor of computer science at the University of Crete, Greece, who studied physics. His research has been recognised with ERC grants, and [a company he co-founded was acquired by Cisco]. He has been practising the Feldenkrais Method for more than five years.

movesim grew out of his love for the Feldenkrais Method, the impact he has seen it have on his own life, and a wish to give something back to the Feldenkrais community.

He sees some of his own path in Feldenkrais's. Moshe Feldenkrais was a physicist and engineer who brought a scientist's curiosity to the human body, and much of his method rests on mechanics: how a push through the foot reaches the head, how the skeleton carries weight so that muscles can let go. movesim follows that thread with today's tools. It started from a simple question, what an instruction actually does in the body, and a physics simulation of the skeleton seemed a fitting way to look at it and to share the answers with people who teach the work.

This is an independent, non-commercial project. [It is not affiliated with any Feldenkrais training or guild.] Comments from practitioners are very welcome, above all where the body does something that does not look right to a trained eye.

Why this exists

In a Feldenkrais lesson, a small movement in one place travels through the whole body: the knees go to one side and the pelvis rolls, the spine twists a little at each level, the head answers. movesim makes that travel visible. It is meant for practitioners who want to see what an instruction does in the skeleton, to explain to others how the work operates, and to try variations while composing lessons.

Students never need to watch a screen. The pictures are for whoever writes, teaches or reviews lessons; in a lesson, attention belongs on the body.
