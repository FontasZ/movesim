The model represents a single reference body and contains no muscles. It is intended for visualisation and teaching; it does not describe any individual and is not a clinical or diagnostic tool.

1. Overview

The model is a rigid multibody system simulated in MuJoCo [1] with a 1 ms time step and implicit integration of joint damping. It comprises 44 rigid segments: the pelvis (free-floating), 24 vertebrae and the skull, a pair of ribs attached to each thoracic vertebra, the shoulder girdle, upper limbs, lower limbs and feet. Each intervertebral joint is represented by three orthogonal hinges (flexion–extension, lateral bending, axial rotation). Muscles are not modelled; joint behaviour arises from passive tissue properties, joint limits and a minimal postural tone.

2. Model description

2.1 Geometry and inertial properties

Bone geometry is taken from BodyParts3D [2], a CT-derived whole-body model of an adult male. Segment lengths are derived from joint landmarks on that skeleton (intervertebral disc centroids, femoral head, tibial plateau, talus, glenohumeral and elbow centres) and scaled to a stature of 1.76 m. Total mass is 70 kg, distributed across segments according to de Leva [3].

2.2 Joint kinematics

Intervertebral ranges of motion follow the representative values of White and Panjabi [4], with thoracic axial rotation (±5° per level, T1–T9) after the in-vitro data of Wilke et al. [5] and L5–S1 axial rotation (±2°) after the in-vivo MRI data of Fujii et al. [6]. The rest configuration includes a sacral slope of 35°, lumbar lordosis of 40°, thoracic kyphosis of 32° and cervical lordosis of 30°. The knee permits flexion and axial tibial rotation; the talocrural and subtalar axes are inclined after Inman [11].

2.3 Passive joint properties

The passive moment resisting a displacement d from rest at each spinal joint is

M(d) = c · (ea d / r − 1),

where r is the range of that level on the side of displacement. The coefficient c is shared by all levels of a region, so that levels in series yield in proportion to their range. For the lumbar spine, a and the whole-region stiffness at rest were fitted to the quartile stiffnesses of the in-vivo meta-analysis of Watt et al. [7]: flexion a = 2.60, 0.243 N·m/°; lateral bending a = 3.67, 0.198 N·m/°; axial rotation a = 2.33, 0.140 N·m/°. Thoracic levels are assumed to share the lumbar coefficients. Cervical coefficients reproduce the mean axial rotation stiffness of 0.07 N·m/° reported by De Boe et al. [8]. Hip rotational stiffness is 8 N·m/rad, within the range reported in vivo [9, 10]. Remaining limb joints combine a linear stiffness with exponential stiffening over the second half of their range.

2.4 Postural tone

No starting position is maintained by passive tissue alone. Each position therefore adds a joint stiffness toward its posture, set to the smallest value that maintains it (e.g. 8 N·m/rad of hip tone in crook lying; 3000 N·m/rad per spinal level in upright sitting). During intentional movement the reference of this tone follows the intended joint angle.

2.5 Contact

Soft tissue is represented by compliant ellipsoidal and spherical contact surfaces (compliance of approximately 2 cm) over the posterior pelvis, the paravertebral regions, the lateral thorax, the scapulae, the occiput, the limbs and the soles. Contact uses elliptic friction cones with a friction coefficient of 0.9 (1.2 for the soles) and a no-slip solver. The environment comprises a floor and, where required, a chair or a pillow.

2.6 Movement generation

Intentional movement is prescribed on a small number of points of the body, through virtual springs attached to moving targets, or on joint angles, through proportional control. All other degrees of freedom are unactuated, so the response of the rest of the body is determined by gravity, contact and passive joint properties.

4. Validation

Model behaviour is compared with in-vivo measurements after every change to the model. Static checks compute moment–angle relations of joint chains under uniform moment; the trunk-rotation check computes the axial rotation of each lumbar level for a 45° rotation of the trunk relative to a fixed pelvis. Because the reference studies differ in subjects, posture and method, agreement indicates consistency of individual components with published data, not validation of the model as a whole.

5. Limitations

  • No muscles: active control, habitual patterns, co-contraction and effort are not represented; postural tone is a single, position-specific stiffness.
  • A single reference body; inter-individual variation in anthropometry, mobility and stiffness is not represented.
  • Thoracic, shoulder, knee and ankle tissue properties are assumed rather than fitted to data.
  • Intervertebral discs are incompressible, the rib cage does not move with respiration, and the scapula is suspended from the clavicle rather than gliding on the thorax.
  • Self-contact is limited to the lower limbs and between upper and lower limbs.
  • Transitions between positions (rolling, sitting up) are not yet modelled.
