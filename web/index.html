See where a movement goes.

A simulated skeleton that shows how a small movement travels through the whole body: the knees tip to one side and the pelvis rolls, the spine turns a little at each level, the head answers. Made for Feldenkrais practitioners, to look, to explain, and to compose lessons.

What it is for

You choose what moves on purpose. Physics, gravity, the floor and the joints decide where the rest of the body goes.

Look

Watch how far a movement reaches: where the twist fades in the spine, which side of the back lifts from the floor, what the head does.

Explain

Show students and colleagues why the pelvis rolls when the knees go to the side, or how a press through the foot reaches the head.

Compose

Try variations side by side: small or large, the head held or free, chest and head turning together or apart.

How to use it

Pick a movement

From the list above, or the menu at the top of the player.

Play and pause

The button at the bottom or the space bar. Click an instruction on the right to jump to it; ½× slows everything down.

Look from anywhere

Drag to turn the body, scroll or pinch to zoom, or choose side, above, from the feet, from the head.

Follow the green dots

show where a movement is led from. Everything else follows on its own.

Built on research

The skeleton's bones come from CT scans, and its joint ranges and the way the spine, neck and hips resist movement follow published measurements of living people. Every change is checked against those measurements.

Who is behind it

movesim is made by Xenofontas (Fontas) Dimitropoulos, a professor of computer science at the University of Crete, Greece, who studied physics. He has been practising the Feldenkrais Method for more than five years.

It grew out of his love for the method, the impact he has seen it have on his own life, and a wish to give something back to the Feldenkrais community.

A model, not a person: it shows how movement can travel through a reference skeleton. It does not show how any particular person moves or feels, and it is not medical advice.
